CNI is organising a two-day workshop on Combinatorial Games on 29–30 October 2026 at IISc Bengaluru. Highlights from Recent Talks

Ubiquity and Evolution of Wi-Fi
Dr. Ashutosh Gore spoke about how Wi-Fi has evolved across six generations, from Wi-Fi 3 to the upcoming Wi-Fi 8. He discussed technologies such as 4K/8K QAM and UL MU-MIMO, along with the use of mmWave for indoor Wi-Fi and the growing role of Wi-Fi in outdoor networks.

Asymptotics of the Number of Labelled Connected Sparse Multitype Graphs
Prof. Luisa Andreis discussed how connected graphs with different types of vertices can be counted when the graphs are sparse. She presented a probabilistic approach to the problem, connecting the enumeration of these graphs with giant components in random graphs, and discussed how connectivity affects the number of possible multitype graphs.

Beyond KL Divergence: Divergence Tests for Universal Hypothesis Testing
Prof. Jithin Ravi discussed how different measures of divergence can be used for statistical hypothesis testing when the underlying distributions are not fully known. He showed how these divergence-based tests perform in one- and two-sample testing, and how their accuracy compares with established methods based on KL and Jensen–Shannon divergence.

Wireless Powered Communications in the Era of 6G
Prof. Ioannis Krikidis discussed how wireless signals can be used to transfer both information and energy, offering a way to support energy-constrained devices without relying entirely on batteries. The talk covered the principles of wireless-powered communication, different network architectures, and the challenges of bringing these ideas into future 6G systems.

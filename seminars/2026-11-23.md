Topic_abstract: "Large language model agents are evolving from single-turn assistants into long-running collaborators that plan, act and coordinate over extended horizons. Yet their effectiveness increasingly depends less on raw reasoning power and more on the machinery around it: what they retain from experience, how they acquire and invoke reusable capabilities, and how they exchange information with humans and other agents. This talk takes a holistic view of these challenges across the full agentic stack, from how knowledge and capabilities are represented, to how they are retrieved and selected in context, to how agents decide and act on them. Drawing on recent research in agent memory, skill frameworks and agentic communication, I will discuss principled ways to make agents more efficient, adaptable and reliable as they scale. I will also connect today's LLM agents to earlier ideas in human-guided learning and planning, and close with open questions toward self-improving, trustworthy multi-agent systems."

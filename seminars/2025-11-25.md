Topic_abstract: |-
  In this talk we will mainly focus on a recent work where we consider a market consisting of many servers who behave like individual agents. Tasks (or jobs) enter this market with a certain valuation and are matched to two servers who each bid a price. If the minimum of the two prices is below the value of the task, the task 
accepts the service from the lowest bidder. This problem can be formulated in terms of a game with a large number of players (agents/servers) who wish to individually maximize their revenue. We examine the dynamics of this game using a mean field game framework and talk about the insights gained through this analysis. We will end the talk with a look at other examples where similar ideas can be exploited to compute distributed control strategies that are Nash-Equilibria.

He earned his Ph.D. from Stanford University in 2016, followed by a Postdoctoral Associate position at MIT. In 2018, he joined UCSD as an Assistant Professor and was promoted to a tenured position in 2022. His research focuses on designing and prototyping advanced systems for wireless communication, sensing, and networking, with applications spanning privacy, security, robotics, health, and everyday life. His work has driven new research directions across fields such as communication theory, RFIC design, and robotics. Many of his innovations have been successfully translated into startups and commercial products, including Haila, Kumu Networks, and Totemic Labs. Under his leadership, the WCSNG group has secured over $15 million in funding from various sources, including the NSF, industry partners, and DoD grants, over the past five years. This funding supports a vibrant team of over 40 students at any given time, helping to train the next generation of engineers and scientists. He has received numerous prestigious accolades for his contributions to wireless research, including being named to Forbes 30 Under 30 (Science) and MIT Technology Review’s Innovators Under 35 list.

Topic_abstract: Wireless sensing offers the promise of enabling transformative applications across robotics, AR/VR, the metaverse, health monitoring, and daily life. Unlike traditional sensors, wireless systems can operate in non-line-of-sight conditions and in inclement weather, providing a complementary, low-cost modality to most other sensing methods. Despite two decades of research, real-world deployments of wireless sensing have remained limited, primarily due to challenges in achieving robust, accurate, and low-overhead implementations. In this talk, I will present a set of wireless sensing systems that tightly integrate with robotics, moving us closer to deployable solutions. First, I will describe how autonomous robots can be used to map both the physical environment and existing wireless infrastructure, enabling robust indoor localization even in challenging corner cases. I will then demonstrate how wireless signals, such as Wi-Fi, can be utilized as an alternative to LiDAR for SLAM, enabling robots to navigate using readily available infrastructure. Building on this, I will highlight how AI-driven wireless radars can enable reliable 3D perception in all weather conditions, enhancing autonomous safety. Finally, I will showcase recent innovations in batteryless wireless force sensors that can be embedded into constrained robotic environments—such as soft robots and wearable haptics—where traditional wired sensing is impractical. These advances demonstrate how robotics and wireless sensing can mutually empower each other to achieve resilient, scalable, and cost-effective autonomy in the real world.

Topic_abstract: "Traditional regret minimization in multi-armed bandits focuses on maximizing cumulative reward, often overlooking fairness across individuals receiving outcomes. Motivated by applications such as clinical trials and resource allocation, Nash regret has been proposed as a fairness-aware metric based on the geometric mean of rewards. In this talk, I will present recent results showing that near-optimal Nash regret can be achieved using simple and general bandit algorithms under mild assumptions in stochastic bandits, along with extensions to a broader class of power-mean fairness objectives. I will then discuss fairness in linear bandits, where we obtain the first order-optimal Nash regret bounds in dimension and introduce a generic meta-algorithm that converts standard linear bandit methods into fairness-aware versions with provable guarantees. Empirical results demonstrate consistent improvements over existing approaches. Overall, the work highlights that fairness can be incorporated into bandit learning without sacrificing statistical efficiency."

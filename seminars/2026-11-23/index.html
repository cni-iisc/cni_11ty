Building Agents That Last: Memory, Skills and Communication Across the Agentic Stack

Dr. Mayukh Das, Principal Researcher, M365 Research, Microsoft

#328
Slides
Abstract

Large language model agents are evolving from single-turn assistants into long-running collaborators that plan, act and coordinate over extended horizons. Yet their effectiveness increasingly depends less on raw reasoning power and more on the machinery around it: what they retain from experience, how they acquire and invoke reusable capabilities, and how they exchange information with humans and other agents. This talk takes a holistic view of these challenges across the full agentic stack, from how knowledge and capabilities are represented, to how they are retrieved and selected in context, to how agents decide and act on them. Drawing on recent research in agent memory, skill frameworks and agentic communication, I will discuss principled ways to make agents more efficient, adaptable and reliable as they scale. I will also connect today's LLM agents to earlier ideas in human-guided learning and planning, and close with open questions toward self-improving, trustworthy multi-agent systems.


Bio
Dr. Mayukh Das, Principal Researcher, M365 Research, Microsoft

Dr. Mayukh Das is a Principal Researcher in M365 Research group at Microsoft, where his work focuses on agentic AI, spanning agent memory, skills, reasoning, reinforcement learning and inter-agent communication. Some of his recent research involves robust scalable memory framework for long-horizon agents (ICML 2026), and learned message representations for agentic communication (ACL 2026). Earlier at Microsoft, he worked on AI-Ops, sequential decision-making and model-efficiency optimization, with publications at NSDI, ISCA, MLSys and CIKM. Before joining Microsoft, he was a Senior Chief Engineer at Samsung R&D Institute, working on neural architecture search and efficient deep-learning inference. He holds a Ph.D. in Computer Science from the University of Texas at Dallas, where he studied human-guided learning and planning with Prof. Sriraam Natarajan. He serves as an Action Editor for the ECML PKDD Journal Track and on program committees of AAAI, ICML, NeurIPS and ICLR.

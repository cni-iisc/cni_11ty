  2. Monotonicity: If \(P\set{X \ge Y} = 1\) and \(\E[Y]\) is well defined with \(\E[Y] > -\infty\), then \(\E[X]\) is well defined and \(\E[X] \ge \E[Y]\).

  3. Functions of random variables: Let \(g: \R \to \R\) be a Borel measurable function, then \(g(X)\) is a random variable with \(\E[g(X)] = \int_{x\in \R}g(x)dF(x)\).

  4. Continuous random variables: Let \(f_X: \R \to [0, \infty)\) be the density function, then \(\E X = \int_{x \in \R}xf_X(x)dx\).

  5. Discrete random variables: Let \(P_X: \sX \to [0,1]\) be the probability mass function, then \(\E X = \sum_{x \in \sX}xP_X(x)\).

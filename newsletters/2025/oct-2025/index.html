CodeIT 2025: Workshop on Codes, Sequences and Information Theory


The Centre for Networked Intelligence (CNI) is organizing CodeIT 2025, a two-day workshop to be held from 11–12 October, featuring invited talks by distinguished national and international experts, covering recent advances in coding theory, sequence design, and information theory.

The workshop, held in honor of Prof. P. Vijay Kumar, aims to foster scholarly exchange and engage students, researchers, and practitioners working at the intersection of communication and information sciences.

Further details are available on the CNI workshop webpage.

Highlights from Recent Talks


Intelligent Resource Management: from LLM Training to Web Serving


Dr. Rohan Gandhi (Microsoft Research India) discussed efficient use of computing resources through two projects; Atlas and KnapsackLB. Atlas improves GPU utilization for faster LLM training, while KnapsackLB balances web traffic, cutting latency and enhancing system reliability and cost-effectiveness. The talk highlighted how intelligent resource management can make AI systems faster, more reliable, and more cost-effective.


Signaling Techniques for Green Communications


Prof. Ranjan K. Mallik (IIT Delhi) spoke on energy-efficient communication systems using low-power receivers and noncoherent signaling. He compared receiver designs and showed how smart signal processing methods can make wireless communication greener, simpler, and more practical for future networks.


When Privacy meets Partial Information: Privacy-Utility Trade-offs in Bandits


Prof. Debabrata Basu (INRIA France) explained how algorithms can protect sensitive data while learning efficiently. He showed that adding Differential Privacy to bandit models maintains performance, offering new insights into balancing learning efficiency and data protection in modern systems.


Detecting AI Slop in Research and Beyond


Prof. Danish Pruthi (IISc Bangalore) discussed detecting AI-generated and plagiarized research content. He explained his team’s methods, such as watermarking, to identify AI text. The talk sparked conversations on ethics, reliability, and responsible AI use in academic writing.


Decoupling Theorem in Quantum Information Theory: Application and Derandomization


Prof. Aditya Nema (IIT Gandhinagar) explained the decoupling theorem’s role in simplifying quantum states. He presented a new approach using pseudorandom unitaries, showing how derandomization improves the efficiency and scalability of quantum information processing.



Dynamic Content Caching and Delivery using Restless Multi-armed Bandits



Ankita Koley (DESE, IISc) presented caching methods that improve online content delivery. Using a restless multi-armed bandit model and a “wait” option, she proposed near-optimal, cost-effective strategies to balance freshness, cost, and performance in data networks.

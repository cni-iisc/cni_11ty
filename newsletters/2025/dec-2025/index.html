Research Spotlight

A glimpse into exciting new research emerging from the CNI community! This month, we celebrate two impactful works, one from our CNI Fellow and another from CNI faculty member.

  • Multi-Topic Projected Opinion Dynamics for Resource Allocation

By Prashil Wankhede et al., to appear at CDC 2025.

This work proposes a novel game-theoretic opinion-dynamics model explaining how social influence affects resource allocation across multiple topics when agents operate under strict budget constraints—shedding light on coordinated decision-making in networks.

  • Does DQN Learn?

By Aditya Gopalan, Gugan Chandrashekhar & Mallika Thoppe, to appear in IEEE TAC (Apr 2026).

This paper examines the learning behavior of Deep Q-Networks, offering theoretical insights into when and how this foundational reinforcement learning method successfully acquires meaningful policies.

Highlights from Recent Talks

Stein’s Method for Diffusion Approximations in Queueing Theory



Prof. Anton Braverman (Northwestern University) introduced the generator comparison approach of Stein’s method for analyzing stationary distributions of Markov processes. He highlighted its growing impact in queueing theory, especially in rigorously understanding diffusion approximations without relying on coupling-based techniques.



Automated A/B testing with the Upper Confidence Bound

Prof. Koulik Khamaru (Rutgers University) discussed the challenges of statistical inference under adaptive experimentation, illustrating pitfalls in naive A/B testing and bandit settings. He introduced stability as a key principle enabling valid inference, showing how UCB-based algorithms preserve reliability despite adaptive data collection.

Learning Where to Look: Reliable Certificates under Scarce Ground Truth


Prof. Gautam Dasarathy (Arizona State University) presented a framework for reliable two-group inference when verified labels are costly. Drawing on active learning and sequential testing, he showed how adaptive verification can yield statistically sound certificates and improve efficiency, with extensions to actively discovering impactful subpopulations.

Design and Performance Analysis of Medium Access Control (MAC) Protocols for Multipacket Reception


Prof. T. G. Venkatesh (IIT Madras) discussed how emerging physical-layer technologies enabling multipacket reception necessitate redesigned MAC protocols. He traced developments from Aloha and CSMA to modern IEEE 802.11 standards, highlighting how leveraging MPR capabilities is crucial for boosting next-generation wireless network performance.

Future of Mobile: Extreme Connectivity, AI-Native, and Beyond Communications


Dr. Harish Viswanathan (Nokia Bell Labs) outlined emerging directions for 6G, emphasizing extreme connectivity, AI-native network design, and integrated sensing. He discussed advances like extreme MIMO, pervasive AI across protocol layers, and sensing-communication fusion, highlighting opportunities and challenges shaping next-generation mobile systems.



CONGO: Compressive Online Gradient Optimization



Prof. Srinivas Shakkottai (Texas A&M University) introduced CONGO, a framework for zeroth-order online convex optimization that exploits sparse gradients to reduce sampling needs. He showed how compressive sensing yields regret bounds independent of full dimension, with experiments demonstrating strong performance in large-scale queueing and microservices systems.

Signal Processing for Integrated Sensing and Communications


Dr. Kumar Vijay Mishra (U.S. DEVCOM Army Research Lab) discussed emerging approaches to distributed ISAC, emphasizing resource optimization for joint sensing and communication. He highlighted architectures for coexistence at mmWave/THz frequencies and dynamic vehicular environments, showcasing methods like sensor fusion and heterogeneous distributed radar–communication co-design.



A Mean Field Game Approach to Resource Allocation in Communication Systems



Dr. Dheeraj Narasimha (INRIA, CNRS Grenoble) explored a market-style model where servers compete via pricing to attract tasks. Using a mean field game framework, he analyzed revenue-maximizing strategies in large-agent systems and highlighted how such formulations yield distributed control policies approaching Nash equilibria.

Layers as Lenses: A Narrative of Feature Learning in Deep Networks


Prof. Harish Guruprasad Ramaswamy (IIT Madras) proposed a new theory of deep learning, arguing that layers act as “lenses” that reweight inputs, enabling feature emergence through a positive feedback loop under gradient descent. He supported this narrative with analysis and experiments on Deep Linearly Gated Networks.



Engineers Without Borders: An Engineering Approach Towards Controlling Infectious Diseases



Prof. Saswati Sarkar (University of Pennsylvania) showcased how communication engineering principles can guide modeling, control, and learning strategies for infectious disease management. She highlighted applications in testing and vaccination under resource constraints and explored the interplay between disease evolution and public response to interventions.
